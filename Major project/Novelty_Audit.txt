The proposal under review, Learning to Insert: Image-Grounded Cell Recovery from Incomplete
Table Structure Predictions, defines a deliberately narrow intervention in table structure recognition
(TSR). Instead of building another end-to-end table parser, it proposes a recovery stage that observes a
table image together with the surviving cell candidates of a fixed base detector, and learns to insert
additional cell instances only where image evidence and surviving-cell context justify them. The
insertion decoder may output a variable number of new candidates, may explicitly decline to insert, and
may recognize that an apparent vacancy belongs to an existing spanning cell. The author asks whether
this design is novel enough for ICDAR or a Q1 journal, and this report answers that question with a
claim-by-claim audit against the 2023-2026 literature.
The audit verified every 2025-2026 reference that decides the novelty question, then swept the TSR
literature broadly: a curated community repository covering more than 150 methods from 2017 to 2026,
ICDAR 2024/2025/2026 proceedings coverage, the 2026 arXiv output (TableSeq, FastTab, StrucTab,
ConRTF, InstructTable), the historical-record reconstruction line in IJDAR, and the adjacent domains that a
reviewer would raise - missed-detection recovery in object detection, semi-supervised pseudo-label
completion, two-stage DETR query generation, and the vision-language-model (VLM) table parsing wave.
Search phrasing covered missing-cell recovery, candidate insertion, cell hypothesis generation, detection
completion, and vacancy detection variants.
Headline finding: the narrow contribution survives. No published TSR system was found that learns a
variable-cardinality insertion operator conditioned on an incomplete, detector-produced candidate set. What
exists today is rule-based geometric gap filling (the IJDAR 2025 historical-record work infers missing cells
from the alignment of detected cells), structure-recovery heuristics (LGPMA searches and merges empty
cells on its own segmentation), span merging from a complete grid (Formerge), and dynamic query
generation inside primary detectors (DQ-DETR-style TSR, DINO-style two-stage query selection). None of
these conditions a learned decoder on surviving candidates, and none implements an explicit insert / no-insert
/ span-compatible decision. The three novelty pillars - detection-conditioned learned insertion, the three-way
insertion decision, and the controlled-deletion plus natural-omission evaluation package - therefore stand,
provided the paper is written against the closest prior art identified in Chapter 5.
Three risks remain, none of them novelty-fatal. First, the empirical risk: on the clean, canonicalized
PubTables-1M benchmark, a strong geometric completion baseline or simple threshold relaxation could
capture many natural omissions, which would shrink the headline gain; the go/no-go gate in the proposal

WHAT WAS SEARCHED AND HOW
2. Audit Scope and Method
The audit had two stages. The first stage verified the existence and content of the ten 2025-2026 references
that the proposal itself flags as decisive, plus one dataset reference (PubTables-v2). Verification meant
locating the paper on arXiv, Springer, ScienceDirect, or a proceedings site; confirming the author list, venue,
and year; and, for the closest priors, reading the abstract or full text to check that the proposal characterizes
them accurately. This step matters because AI-assisted drafts sometimes cite plausible but nonexistent work,
and any such fabrication would have invalidated the proposal's novelty boundary.
The second stage was a broad novelty sweep anchored on the task definition - learned, variable-cardinality
insertion of cell candidates into an incomplete detector output. Primary sources were: a curated
table-structure-recognition repository maintained by a community researcher, which indexes roughly 150
methods from 2017 to 2026 with venues; ICDAR 2024 and 2025 proceedings entries (SepFormer, TABLET,
DTSM, ETT, MuTabNet, SPRINT, ClusterTabNet, and peers); ICDAR 2026 acceptances visible on arXiv
(ConRTF); 2026 arXiv preprints (TableSeq, FastTab, StrucTab, InstructTable); and the image-to-sequence
and VLM line (TableFormer, VAST, UniTable, TabPedia, InstructTable, StrucTab). Adjacent domains were
swept as well: missed-detection and false-negative recovery in object detection, semi-supervised object
detection with pseudo-label completion (the Soft Teacher line), two-stage DETR and DINO query selection,
and DETR-variance analysis relevant to query design.
Query phrasings included combinations of: missing cell recovery, missed cell detection, cell completion,
candidate insertion, cell hypothesis, detection completion, vacancy detection, empty cell recovery, threshold
relaxation, and proposal omissions, crossed with table structure recognition and with generic object
detection. The IJDAR 2025 historical-record paper was fetched in full to verify the exact mechanism of its
missing-cell inference, because it is the closest direct prior and the proposal's novelty argument leans on
distinguishing learned insertion from that rule-based completion.
Three limitations apply. The sweep relied on web-indexed sources, so paywalled full texts other than the
closest priors were checked at abstract level only. Non-English venues were not systematically covered,

4.1 Pillar 1 - learned, variable-cardinality insertion
The search found no system that takes an incomplete candidate set produced by a base detector and emits a
variable number of new cell instances through a learned decoder conditioned on that set. The rule-based
completion in the IJDAR 2025 historical-record paper is the nearest functional neighbor: it generates
candidate cells where gaps are identified from the horizontal and vertical alignment of detected positive
cells, but it is a fixed geometric heuristic with no learned insertion decision, no image-grounded scoring at
insertion time, and no ability to abstain. LGPMA recovers empty cells, yet it does so inside its own
mask-alignment segmentation pipeline rather than on top of an external detector's candidate set, and its
recovery is a search-and-merge procedure. Query-based detectors such as TATR and DQ-DETR-style TSR
generate cell hypotheses from image content, but as primary detectors - they never observe a surviving
candidate set, and their per-query background class is not a decision about a specific geometric vacancy in a
given incomplete table. This is the gap the proposal fills, and it is a genuine one.

The insert / no-insert / span-compatible outcome space is the most differentiating design element.
Spanning-cell handling exists, but always as a separate operation: split-and-merge methods merge grid
elements into spans (SPLERGE, SEM, Formerge), and LGPMA locates and divides empty cells with rules.
No learned operator was found that decides, per vacancy, whether a new independent cell, no cell, or a
span-compatible explanation is correct. The closest conceptual relative is the background class of a DETR
query, which teaches a query to output nothing, but that is objectness in a primary detector rather than a
structural explanation of a vacancy conditioned on neighboring cells. If the experiments show this three-way
head reduces over-segmentation of spanning cells relative to a box-only ablation, it becomes the paper's most
defensible architectural contribution.
4.3 Pillar 3 - the evaluation package
The controlled-deletion protocol with isolated, adjacent, and span-adjacent deletion patterns; the split of
natural omissions into filtered omissions (a candidate existed below the operating threshold) versus proposal
omissions (no candidate existed even in a generous pre-filter pool); and the insertion-specific metrics
(insertion precision, recovery recall, false insertion rate, candidate recall gain, span violation rate, intact-table
no-change accuracy) together form an evaluation contribution that the sweep did not find elsewhere in TSR.
Robustness-by-deletion appears in detection generally, and competition reports often analyze missed
detections, but the proposal's package is assembled into a task-level protocol with matched-precision
comparison against threshold relaxation and geometric completion. As a standalone contribution it would be
thin; as the experimental backbone of the learned-insertion paper it is exactly what a skeptical reviewer
needs.

DOSSIERS ON THE EIGHT NEAREST NEIGHBORS
5. Closest Prior Art: Deep Analysis
This chapter gives each methodological neighbor a short dossier: what it does, what it leaves open, and what
the future paper must do to differentiate itself. Together these dossiers define the related-work section of the
submission and the baselines a reviewer will expect.
5.1 LGPMA - empty-cell searching and merging (ICDAR 2021)
LGPMA aligns cell regions with local and global pyramid masks and then runs a uniform structure recovery
pipeline of cell matching, empty cell searching, and empty cell merging, which locates and splits empty cells
so that both non-empty and empty cells are handled. It won the ICDAR 2021 best industry paper award and
remains the standard citation for structure-aware vacancy handling. What it leaves open: recovery happens
inside its own segmentation pipeline with rule-driven searching and merging, not as a learned operator over
an external detector's candidate set; there is no explicit abstain outcome and no notion of returning
no-insertion on an intact table. The paper must present LGPMA as the strongest established combination of
image evidence and topological vacancy reasoning, and position insertion as a different intervention point:
after a fixed detector, on its retained candidate set.
5.2 Singh and Middleton (IJDAR 2025) - alignment-based missing-cell
inference
This is the closest direct prior, and the audit verified its mechanism from the full text. The authors state the
procedure explicitly:
Since the trained model generates a limited number of table cells (up to 2000, including both
positive and negative predictions), we implemented a method to infer missing cells based on the
horizontal and vertical alignment of detected positive cells. This approach ensures complete table
reconstruction by generating candidate cells where gaps are identified and aligning them with
existing cells.
Singh and Middleton, IJDAR vol. 28, 2025, Section 3.2
The method is exactly the coordinate-based geometric completion that the proposal demotes to baseline
status: no learned insertion decision, no image evidence at insertion time, no abstention class, and a
historical-records domain with CascadeTabNet as the detector. It establishes that generating candidates at
detected gaps is not new, which the proposal already concedes. For the submission, this paper is
simultaneously a threat and an asset: it is the geometric-completion baseline to reimplement on the same
detector, and it proves the field recognizes missing-cell reconstruction as a real problem worth solving.

5.3 Singh et al. (IJDAR 2025) - semi-supervised historical TSR
The companion paper studies semi-supervised table structure recognition for digitizing historical tables and
evaluates missing-cell generation and reconstruction within that setting. Its relevance is contextual rather than
competitive: it shows the historical-table community actively works on incomplete structure recovery, and it
supplies a domain where omissions are frequent and where a recovery module could later be stress-tested. It
leaves the supervised, detection-conditioned intervention study untouched, and it does not constrain the
novelty claim.
5.4 Formerge - recovering spanning cells from a grid (ICDAR 2023)
Formerge splits a table into a grid of cells via separator detection and then uses a transformer to recover
spanning cells from that grid. Operationally it is a merging operator: it reduces the cardinality of a complete
grid into spans, whereas the proposed insertion operator increases the cardinality of an incomplete set. The
reason it matters here is design vocabulary: Formerge demonstrates that learned reasoning about which
regions belong to a common spanning cell is feasible and publishable, which directly supports the plausibility
of the span-compatible outcome. The paper should cite it as the span-side counterpart and explain that
span-compatibility in the insertion setting means abstaining from subdividing a region that an existing span
already explains.
5.5 GrabTab - flexible component deliberation (AAAI 2024)
GrabTab treats complex table recognition as progressive deliberation over flexible components, selecting
reasonable structural components rather than forcing a fixed representation. It is cited here because it
normalizes the idea that TSR benefits from explicit decision-making about structure, not just prediction. It
does not insert candidates into an incomplete detection set and does not threaten the claim, but it belongs in
the related work on structural decision-making, alongside TRACE and the separator-regression family
(TSRFormer, SepFormer, SEMv3), which collectively show the field moving toward decomposed,
decision-heavy TSR pipelines.
5.6 DQ-DETR-style TSR and TSRFormer - dynamic queries in
primary detectors
A Pattern Recognition 2023 paper applies Dynamic Queries Enhanced DETR to robust table structure
recognition, and TSRFormer (ACM MM 2022) uses separator-focused two-stage transformer detection.
Both generate queries from image content rather than using a static query bank, which is mechanistically
similar to the proposal's image-attention insertion queries. The difference is conditioning and role: these are
primary detectors that produce the full candidate set from the image alone, without observing any surviving
candidate set, and without a per-vacancy structural decision. The submission must cite both and state the
distinction plainly: dynamic query generation is established; dynamic query generation conditioned on an
incomplete candidate set, with insert/no-insert/span-compatible outcomes, is not.

5.7 DINO two-stage query selection and semi-supervised pseudo-label
completion
Two mechanism relatives live outside TSR and should be cited preemptively. DINO (ICLR 2023)
popularized mixed query selection, initializing decoder queries from encoder proposals - i.e., queries
anchored on candidate content. Semi-supervised object detection (the Soft Teacher line and successors)
completes missing pseudo-labels for unlabeled data during training, which is functionally learned completion
of a retained set's missing boxes, but at training time and without any table-structural reasoning. Neither
performs inference-time, structure-aware expansion of a detector's output set. Citing both converts a
potential reviewer objection - is this not just two-stage DETR or SSL? - into evidence that the mechanism is
sound and the task setting is new.
5.8 The VLM and image-to-sequence line
From TableFormer (CVPR 2022) through VAST (CVPR 2023), UniTable (2024, whose authors report
surpassing GPT-4o and GPT-4-turbo with vision on table parsing), TabPedia (NeurIPS 2024), UniTabNet
(EMNLP 2024), InstructTable (2026), and StrucTab (ECCV 2026), autoregressive models emit structure
tokens and coordinates in one stream. In a loose sense every emitted cell is an insertion, but there is no
explicit detection-conditioned insertion stage: the candidate set is not an input, no-insertion is not an
outcome, and span-compatibility is implicit in HTML attributes. Cell box precision from sequence decoders
also typically trails dedicated detection heads, which is precisely why a detection-level recovery module
remains relevant. StrucTab deserves a sentence of its own: with intermediate structural supervision,
decomposed RL rewards, and a new benchmark, it represents the strongest current VLM-side claim, and the
submission should compare against or at least discuss it.
5.9 The residual gap the paper may claim
No published TSR system learns a variable-cardinality insertion operator that expands an
incomplete, detector-produced candidate set using raster image evidence and surviving-cell
context, with an explicit learned decision among insertion, abstention, and span-compatibility.
Geometric completion, vacancy searching, span merging, dynamic queries, and training-time
label completion each exist in isolation; their detection-conditioned, decision-aware composition
does not.
Residual gap statement supported by this audit - re-verify immediately before submission

WHAT REVIEWERS WILL ATTACK
6. Threat Analysis and Positioning
6.1 Threat 1 - is this not just LGPMA or the IJDAR reconstruction?
This is the most likely first-round objection, and the answer is already half-built into the proposal's novelty
table. The differentiation rests on four properties the priors lack: the operator is learned end to end; it is
conditioned on an external detector's surviving candidate set; its output cardinality varies; and it can
explicitly abstain or defer to an existing span. The submission should make this a single sharp paragraph plus
a comparison table, and then let the matched-precision experiment against a reimplementation of
alignment-based completion carry the argument empirically. Anything asserted in prose but not measured
against that baseline will be discounted.
6.2 Threat 2 - lower the threshold or improve the detector instead
Detector-side improvement remains a live alternative: ConRTF at ICDAR 2026 gains up to +1.6 GriTS over
real-time detector baselines purely through training-time edge weighting, and threshold relaxation requires
no new components at all. The proposal already schedules the lower-threshold baseline; the submission
should also report a candidate-budget-matched comparison (same number of retained candidates for both
methods) and discuss the failure signature of thresholds - duplicates and competing false candidates rather
than clean recovery. If learned insertion only wins where thresholds fail, say so explicitly; that is a stronger,
more honest claim than a blanket one.
6.3 Threat 3 - why not just use a vision-language model?
The VLM wave is the biggest contextual change since the proposal was drafted: UniTable reports beating
GPT-4o on table parsing, StrucTab is an ECCV 2026 system with decomposed RL rewards, and
InstructTable shows instruction-guided multi-stage TSR. The submission needs a positioning paragraph that
concedes the trend and then draws the line: the proposed module operates at the detection level where precise
cell geometry is decided; sequence models are optimized for structure and content tokens and their boxes
typically trail dedicated detectors; the recovery module is orthogonal and attachable to any detector,
including one inside a VLM pipeline; and it introduces no generative content beyond verified
image-grounded boxes. If compute allows, one sequence-model comparison on PubTables-1M cell
localization, or a cited number from prior work, closes this thread decisively.
6.4 Threat 4 - synthetic deletion is not detector failure
The proposal handles this correctly by making natural omissions the deciding evaluation, with synthetic
deletion only for controlled analysis. Two additions will harden it: first, report the omission taxonomy of the
real detector (how many omissions are filtered versus proposal omissions, and their size, position, and
span-adjacency distribution) so reviewers can judge ecological validity; second, include the intact-table
